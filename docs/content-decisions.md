# Content and design decisions

This file holds the reasoning behind specific pages, products and layouts: what the user picked, what they rejected and why. It was moved out of `AGENTS.md` on 2026-10-07 to keep the always-loaded agent guide short; the rules themselves are summarised there. **Read the matching section here before editing a product entry, the product panel, a service photo, the footer, the About page or the home pages.** Like `AGENTS.md`, it is written in English; dates are absolute.

## Services showcase

**The services design is the photo showcase the user picked on a design canvas** (2026-08-29, "Yön D"): the listing is full-width alternating strips — `ServiceStrip.astro`, one per service, `surface-muted` band + red number + summary + photo, photo side alternating — and the detail page follows the same language (red index number in the hero, top-ruled scope columns, a `surface-muted` approach band with red step numbers). The photos live in `src/assets/services/<id>.jpg` (Pexels, free commercial license — sources and the no-visible-brands rule are in that folder's README.md) and each service's `imageAlt` lives per-language in `services.ts`; a service id with no matching photo renders as a single text column.

**CSS-drawn mockups and self-playing decorative animation are rejected here** (2026-08-29, two rounds before the photo direction settled): the user had hand-drawn CSS mockups and their micro-animations removed root and all. Real photography (or user-supplied media) is the accepted visual style; don't reintroduce drawn mockups or autoplaying motion, and run visual direction changes past the user on a canvas first. Motion tied to the visitor's own action is a different matter — see the home process section under Home page below, which the user asked for.

## Product panels

**Product teasers are product-coloured panels** (`src/components/ProductPanel.astro`). This is design "H", which the user picked on the products canvas on 2026-09-23. They had said the earlier full-width strips did not look clickable, and they rejected variations that only restyled the button. Each product is one rounded panel, and the whole panel is a single link.

- **Colour:** the panel sits on the product's own colour, frontmatter `color` (a `#rrggbb` taken from the product's own visuals, neutral `#262626` when unset). The schema rejects a colour whose contrast with white is under 7:1 (`contrastWithWhite` in `src/utils/color.ts`), which leaves room for the 80 % white secondary text.
- **Text column:** the status chip (its dot green for a live product; the user's call on 2026-09-16, because it reports a state, not identity), the `category · platforms` line, title, tagline, `rating` (Rating's `tone="inverse"`) and `results`, and a white pill button carrying the page's `linkLabel`. The pill is white rather than the site's blue because blue clashes on a coloured ground; the panel itself is the click target.
- **Visuals:** they bleed off the panel edge. A portrait cover stands as a phone, with the product's first portrait feature image as a second phone behind it from `lg:` up. A landscape cover slides in from the right. **Below `md:` the panel is compact** (2026-09-28, after the user saw a panel fill a whole phone screen: "çok büyük"): type one step smaller, and the visual shares the last row with the button instead of getting its own band — a portrait cover peeks in as a 112px-wide phone at the bottom-right corner beside the results and the pill, a landscape cover becomes a 160px band under the button that bleeds off the bottom-right (`object-top` for a screenshot so its heading reads, `object-center` for a photo so its subject shows). This is done by making the text column `display: contents` below `md:` so its children sit directly in the panel's two-column grid; only the results+button block is `col-start-1` and the phone cell `col-start-2`, so they land in the same row by auto-placement. That block is `self-end` in the grid and must stay `md:self-stretch` in the flex column, where `self-end` would push it to the right edge. Verified with headless-Chrome screenshots at 390 px, not on the canvas; the desktop layout is unchanged.
- **Where it's used:** both home pages show the panels under a red "Ürünlerimiz" label with an outlined "Tüm ürünler" button. Both product listings mark their first panel `eager`, because it can be the LCP element. The panel and its visuals lift on hover only under `motion-safe`.

## Product entries

### İbadet Rehberim

**The İbadet Rehberim entry is deliberately thin.** It is not a sales page for the app (ibadetrehberim.com already is one); its job is to show a prospective client "we built this, this is how". So it intentionally has no `faq`, no `testimonials`, no `rating` (only the verifiable download count in `results`) and only two of the app's features — the ones that show engineering decisions. Don't fill those sections in to make the page "complete"; it was published on 2026-09-25 without an App Store link (the app is not on the App Store yet, so `stores` carries only Google Play and web, with no `pending` placeholder); add `stores.appStore` when it ships.

### QR Menü

**QR Menü (`qr-menu`) is the studio's own QR menu product for restaurants and cafés, sold through webmenu.info** (added 2026-09-28). It follows the İbadet Rehberim pattern: `client: "Kendi ürünümüz"`, `stores.web` only, `pricing.label` naming the three packages (Silver · Gold · Premium). The site publishes no prices, so there is deliberately no `app` block — the schema never carries a price we don't know. `results` is empty (no verified business count) and there are no `testimonials`: the three reviews on webmenu.info are unverified and would enter the schema. It is the `caseStudy` of the `web` service. Its `logo` is an SVG redrawn from the 97 px PNG on webmenu.info (measured pixel by pixel, same purple; there was no larger source), and its `cover` is a Pexels café-table photo cropped to 3:2 and **mirrored** so the QR card sits at the left edge — the panel clips the right quarter of a landscape cover, and unmirrored the card fell into the clipped part. Sources and the rejected candidates (readable venue names, POS brands) are in `src/content/projects/_images/README.md`. There are no feature images: phone captures of a live menu go into `features[].image` if they arrive. **The page is deliberately a short summary** (the user's call, 2026-09-28, after a first draft with five sections and six features: "insanlar okumuyorlar"): two body paragraphs, three features of at most two sentences each, one link to webmenu.info for everything else — no package table, no setup steps, no "why not a template" argument. Don't expand it. Only Gold and Premium include a business-specific design (Silver is a standard one), so the copy says the menu is designed for the business "on request" rather than as a blanket claim.

### Dernek Asistan

**Dernek Asistan is a case study that names no client** (`type: case-study`, an internal staff tool). On 2026-09-23 the user asked that the client organisation not be named anywhere in the project, this file included. So the entry has no `client` field and the copy only says that charities use the system; the product page's künye then shows just the sector. There are no `stores`, no `pricing`, and deliberately no `app` block, so the page emits no `SoftwareApplication` schema — reporting an offer and install URL for software nobody can obtain would be fiction. `results` is empty because there are no verified usage figures; the prose describes the benefits qualitatively instead. Its only image is the cover, `_images/dernek-asistan/cover.png`: a composed promo visual the user supplied on 2026-09-21 (laptop dashboard, form and report cards on a desk, sample figures) that replaced all per-feature screenshots — the user asked for this one image and nothing else, so the `features` have no `image` and the old screen captures were deleted; don't re-add feature screenshots here. The figures in it are sample data, so the entry keeps `screenshotsIllustrative: true`, which prints the `product.illustrative` note ("Görüntüdeki veriler temsilidir.") under the cover. If screenshots ever return, capture at DPR 2 — see the product-image rule in `AGENTS.md`, which this entry's screenshots broke once already.

## Case-study copy

**Case-study copy is written for the client's manager, not for an engineer** (the user's call, 2026-09-04, tightened again 2026-09-21): short, benefit-first, no implementation vocabulary and no screen-level detail either. The prose answers "how does this make the work easier, what got better about tracking and reporting", not "how did we build it" or "which fields does the form have". The Dernek Asistan body went first from a "Nasıl geliştirdik" section with five technical subsections to problem → what changed → today, and then (2026-09-21, after the user read "Çalışan bağışçının adını ve bağış türünü giriyor; fiyatı, tutarı ve sıra numarasını..." and called it needless detail) to problem → today only, with the benefits carried by the `features`: each feature `title` is a benefit headline (time saved, everything on record, easier tracking, consistent data, one-click reports, more donations with the same team), each `text` is at most two sentences, and step-by-step descriptions of the UI ("tabs", "fields", "the form is filled once") are out. Naming the stack, the schema or the enforcement layer belongs in a blog post if anywhere; describing form fields belongs nowhere on the page. Claims stay qualitative because there are no verified figures (see `results`).

## Footer

**The footer is four columns plus a copyright row** (modelled on a competitor footer the user showed on 2026-09-21): brand (Logo + wordmark, the `footer.tagline` string, round Instagram and WhatsApp buttons), pages, services (every entry in `services.ts`, so all four service pages get a site-wide internal link) and contact (e-mail and phone as icon rows in `brand` red, from `contact.ts`; WhatsApp sits with the social buttons, not here). The copyright row's right side holds the other-language link (`alternate` is passed through from `BaseLayout`, falling back to the other home page like the header switcher) — not privacy/KVKK links, because the site has no cookies and no form yet, so those pages don't exist; add them there when the contact form or analytics lands. `sm:` shows two columns with the brand block spanning both, `md:` the full four.

## About page

- **The About page argues from principles, never from figures** (written 2026-09-23 after surveying ~19 studio/agency About pages, TR and international). It has six sections — hero → "Projelerde öngörülebilirlik" (+ photo) → "Çalışma ilkelerimiz" → "Kendi ürünlerimiz" → "Hizmetlerimiz" → CTA — and runs ~500 words, which is where the credible small-studio pages sit. Four rules hold it together, and each of them is a thing the surveyed pages got wrong: **no counts, no founding year and no mention of being young** (the same ban as the product hero; the pages that announce youth read as riskier, not fresher, and one of them listed "uygun fiyat" as a company value); **no Misyon/Vizyon pair**, which is the tell of every weak Turkish page; **every principle is a behaviour a competitor could plausibly contradict** ("Bağımlılık oluşturmayız", "Muhatabınız, projeyi geliştiren ekiptir"), not an adjective like "şeffaflık" or "kalite"; and **proof is a checkable artefact, not an aggregate** — the page points at the published products, the blog and this site itself instead of counting anything.
- The six principles are deliberately *not* the home page's four process steps: home says what happens in a project and in what order, About says why we work that way. Keep that split — restating the stages here would make one of the two pages redundant. The commitments are also the same promises `services.ts` and the home steps make, so a sentence that will not appear in a real proposal does not belong in the list.
- The page emits an `AboutPage` block through the `head` slot whose `mainEntity` is `{ '@id': organizationId(...) }` — it points at the layout's Organization rather than restating the company, the same rule as every other page-level schema.
- **The English page omits the blog link that the Turkish one carries.** `/en/blog/` is deliberately empty (see the language-asymmetry note in `AGENTS.md`), and sending an English reader to an empty listing as evidence would undercut the exact paragraph it sits in. The rest of the two pages match section for section.
- Its one photo is `src/assets/about/planning.jpg` (Pexels, source in that folder's README), rendered in the `ServiceStrip` idiom. Inner pages open with a *text* hero — the photo hero belongs to the home page — so the red uppercase label above the `h1` stands in for the red index number the service pages use.

## Home page

- **The home hero is layout "K" with photo "R2"** (both picked on the hero design canvas, 2026-09-01): a two-column grid on `md:` — left-aligned heading, copy and buttons on the left, `src/assets/home/meeting-room.jpg` (an empty glass-walled meeting room, Pexels #13323677, source in that folder's README) on the right as a `rounded-2xl` bordered, shadowed 4:3 card capped at 480px and rendered `eager`/`fetchpriority="high"` at width 960 because it is the LCP candidate. Below `md:` the card drops under the text. The user's reference for the photo was an empty modern meeting room with laptops on the table; keep that register (no people, no readable brands) if the photo is ever swapped.
- **The home hero background is "Yön H"** from the same canvas (picked out of nine directions): a cone-shaped `action`-blue spot from the top, a sparse dot texture fading toward the edges, and a soft-light grain layer. All three are static `aria-hidden` divs inside the `relative overflow-hidden` hero section, styled by `.hero-dots` / `.hero-spot` / `.hero-grain` in `global.css` (mask-image and a data-URI can't be written as Tailwind classes). Colours come from tokens, and the spot's intensity is `--hero-spot` (24% light, 22% dark — light needs more because the blue washes out on white) next to the logo variables. Both home pages carry the same three divs — change them in both. It is decoration without motion, so it sits inside the no-autoplaying-motion rule.
- **The home process section** ("Çalışma sürecimiz") is a sticky intro column (label, heading, CTA, the team photo from `src/assets/home/team.jpg` — Pexels, source in that folder's README) beside a red-numbered vertical timeline whose guide line fills red as the visitor scrolls. The fill is `.process-fill` in `global.css`, driven by `animation-timeline: view()` — it moves only when the page moves, which is why it is allowed under the no-decorative-motion rule; browsers without scroll timelines and reduced-motion visitors get the plain grey line. The user asked for this explicitly (2026-08-29), and the layout was modelled on an agency site they liked. The `steps` array (title, thesis, text) lives in each home page's frontmatter; the copy makes promises (timeline and cost in writing, maintenance after launch) that must stay true, so don't sharpen them for effect. **No fixed demo or release interval may be promised anywhere.** The site said "a working version every two weeks" in the hero, this step, the About page and a blog post until 2026-09-23, when the user pointed out that the team does not work to that cadence. The development step now says the client witnesses the progress ("İlerlemeye siz de şahit olursunuz", the user's own wording), and the hero says the client can follow it.
